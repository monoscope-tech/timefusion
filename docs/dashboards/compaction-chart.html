Six sealed days are bit-identical 58 hours later, and so is the whale history that was supposed to be eating the budget. Snapshot v496820 against v490774. Every cell from 08-13 through 08-18 reads exactly what it read on 08-20 — 08-13 at 35·32·2·47·43, 08-16 at 2·20·1·35·1, cell for cell — across many deploys. The single change in the band went the wrong way: 6297304f / 08-17 grew 274 → 275. It is still the richest cell on the page at 304 files/GB, it was named as such on the 08-20 revision, and it has never been claimed.

The correction this snapshot forces. The 08-20 page concluded that oldest-first was spending the sealed budget on the whale’s 07-20…08-02 history — 1,083 files, 760 GB, 0.42 files saved per GB. That tail is also byte-for-byte unchanged: 30, 70, 116, 113, 121, 33, 76, 70, 104, 53, 103, 82, 80, 32. If capacity were being spent rewriting those partitions, their file counts would have moved. They did not. So the budget is not going to the low-yield population and landing; it is going there and never completing — which is exactly what the 08-20 task-start sample showed and this page under-weighted at the time: 30 of 31 sealed starts already on attempts > 3, whale 07-30 climbing attempts 1 → 7 at 4.44 GB per unit against a 900 s deadline. Re-claims of work that keeps timing out produce no file-count change anywhere, on either side of the yield split. The ordering defect is real and worth fixing; it is not sufficient on its own, because fixing what gets picked does not help if nothing that gets picked finishes.

What did move, and it is the whole tell. Only 08-19, the newest sealed day at the previous snapshot, drained: 28f62f01 413 → 56 and whale 277 → 238. 08-20 and 08-21 have since sealed and sit at 42·49·35 and 32·30·47 — fragmented, not converged. The pattern across two snapshots is that a day gets one shot while it is the frontier, and whatever it fails to finish it keeps forever. That makes the frozen band widen by one day per day, which is what six consecutive identical days means.

Where the remaining debt actually is. Table-wide there are 4,374 active files over 1,032 GB in 1,070 project-days, and 983 of those cells hold two files or fewer. The entire problem is about twenty cells, and four of them carry it:

Why this page matters to query latency. The same fragmentation is the read path’s cost: scan construction runs at roughly 14.5 µs per selected file, so a 275-file cell is pure planning tax on every query that touches 08-17. It is also why the tantivy prefilter throws away most of its work — 76% of prefilter skips are no_index_or_cap, i.e. the index is not there, and per-file indexing means each of these 275 files needs its own. Compaction, index coverage and plan cost are one problem.

The shape of the fix, unchanged from 08-20 and still unwritten. Rank sealed candidates by estimated files-removed per byte-rewritten rather than by age, keeping a starvation floor; widen the converged test from “at or above target” to a band around it so a 0.52 GB file against a 1 GB target is left alone. Add to it, on this snapshot’s evidence: a unit that has exceeded its deadline several times must be split or shelved rather than re-claimed, or the ordering fix simply re-aims a pipeline that never delivers. This page still documents a diagnosis, not a shipped fix.

Superseded, kept for the reasoning chain. Written against the 08-20 snapshots. Two conclusions below are now falsified by measurement: that 2efbcf5 would unfreeze 08-13 (it did not), and that the sealed budget was being consumed rewriting the whale history (its file counts are unchanged, so it was not).

The backlog this page was built to track is gone. Widening the window past 08-12 for the first time shows 944 project-days holding 2,168 files between them, and 900 of those 944 cells sit at two files or fewer. Only 15 carry ten or more, and 14 of the 15 are not fragmented at all — they are the whale’s 07-20…08-02, running 0.42–1.0 GB per file against a 1 GB target. A 91 GB day should be 116 files. The single genuinely fragmented historical cell in the entire tail is whale / 08-09, 11 files for 0.9 GB. Every remaining problem on this page lives in the last seven days.

08-13 is starved by partitions that need no work at all. It has read exactly 167 for four snapshots, six days after sealing, and the cause is now measured rather than inferred. Over 25 minutes of prod task starts: 20 SealedConsolidation claims, 11 of them on whale 07-30 / 07-31 / 08-01, and zero on 08-13. Whale 07-30 alone was claimed 7 times with attempts climbing 1 → 7, at 4.44 GB estimated per unit, exceeding its 900 s deadline every time. Those partitions are already correctly packed — 103 files at 0.52 GB each against a 1 GB target.

The defect is in candidate selection, and it is the tag mistake this codebase has now made twice. select_coordinator_compaction_candidates gated its converged-file skip behind !has_unsorted, and is_sorted_run reads a tag only the OPTIMIZE path writes — 1,593 of 1,648 prod add actions carry none. So has_unsorted was true for effectively every partition and if add.size >= target { continue } was unreachable: a partition admitted for holding two small files then selected its half-gigabyte converged ones and re-sorted them. The admission test was already fixed to be size-only for exactly this reason (“SIZE only. Sortedness belongs to Repair”); selection never got the same fix. Shipped as 2efbcf5.

The ordering fix was working — that is what exposed this. Oldest-first starvation landed at 21:15 and correctly aimed sealed capacity at the oldest debt; the oldest debt just happened to be fourteen whale days that need nothing. Which also retires the hypothesis this page published earlier today: 08-13 is not cursed by its date. 08-14 (52), 08-15 (47) and 08-16 (67) converged because they were reached before the ordering changed, and 08-13 is simply not the oldest thing in the queue.

Where a day does start, it finishes. 08-18 has gone 979 → 798 → 501 across three snapshots, and the open partition’s smaller tenants collapsed in the same six hours (98fdd4f3 117 → 19, be87ebc1 65 → 17, d062e010 45 → 21). The mechanism works; what fails is selection. 08-17 is the counter-example still outstanding: 396 → 381, with 6297304f holding 274 of them and moving by a single file in twelve hours.

The rollup stall behind this has been fixed. Base rollup peaked at 88,618 pending units and is now ~7,600. One defect caused it: the byte estimate excluded only files entirely disjoint from a slice, then counted every remaining file whole. Maintenance files span a day, so a ten-minute slice estimated exactly what a day-wide slice did — and split_time_task, which bisects whatever “does not fit”, produced halves estimating the same, eleven times over: 87,000 units across 204 real cells, 427 per cell, 26,109 of them at the one-minute floor. Fixed by scaling each file’s contribution to the share of its own span the slice covers.

A caution about this page’s own numbers. Earlier today a companion tool reported shipbubble as 0/14 days sorted. It was wrong: pyarrow’s S3 client sends x-amz-checksum-mode, which OVH rejects, so every footer read failed — and the tool summed None as false. Re-measured, it is 9/9. The counts here come from Delta Add actions and are unaffected, but the lesson generalises: a three-valued probe must never be folded into a boolean at the summing step, or a broken measurement reads as a broken system.

On reading these numbers. Counts are active Adds in the Delta snapshot. An aws s3 ls of the same table returns 24,637 objects and suggests 350–600 files per project on 08-16 — that listing is dominated by tombstones not yet vacuumed, and the active count for those same partitions is 1–35. Worth naming because the physical listing is the easier measurement to reach for and it is wrong by an order of magnitude.
