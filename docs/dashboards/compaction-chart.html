How to read the grid. On a sealed day a healthy cell holds about ceil(GB / 0.5) files: one file for each small tenant, two to four for the three largest. The open partition always carries many small files and most of the deletion-vector files, because flushes and hot packing are still writing it; judge it by whether its deletion-vector count falls through the day.

Deletion vectors. A deletion-vector file makes every read of it load and filter the masked rows. The strip lane rewrites those files without the masked rows, at a fleet-wide cap of 12 per 10 minutes so fresh uncached files cannot flood the read path. Sealed days should show none; a date named in the first tile still holds some.

The then/now row keeps the 08-28 snapshot as a fixed baseline for the 08-24 fragmentation cliff; the bottom row is regenerated on every refresh.

Reading this section. The tiles and table are regenerated from monoscope's hourly metrics on every refresh. "CPU with ≥300 tasks due" asks whether the box works harder when work piles up: if that median sits well under 48 cores while the token row is at its ceiling, the token budget, not the CPU, is what limits the drain. Tokens are priced per 64 MiB decoded (d9f00cce), so they cap I/O in flight, deliberately, after day-wide rebuild scans starved client queries at the object store.

What to compare. CPU vs state-memory admission refusals shows which budget binds; the live section above shows the same for the current process. Rollup hit rate and certification skip rate are the read-path payoff of maintenance: if compaction drops duplicates but those two stay low, reads do not benefit yet. The p99 chart is the worst one-minute window in each hour; long flat tops near 60 s are queries hitting the client timeout.
