The packing fix shipped, and the two frozen days did not move a single file. Prod is running 52e99aa, which carries 2efbcf5. 08-13 reads 167 and 08-17 reads 382 — bit-identical to the 23:15 snapshot 5½ hours earlier, cell for cell, with 6297304f still holding exactly 274. A number that repeats to the file across a deploy is not slow progress; it is no progress, and it means the previous diagnosis was incomplete.

What 2efbcf5 actually fixed, and what it left. It made the converged-file skip unconditional: if add.size >= target { continue }. That correctly excludes files at or above the 1 GB target. The whale’s pre-08-03 days are packed at 0.42–1.0 GB per file — every one of them sits just under target, so none are skipped, all are “small”, and the partition is still admitted and still rewritten. The fix removed the pathological case and left the expensive-and-pointless one untouched.

The real defect is that ordering ranks by age and never by benefit. Measure each cell as files removed per GB rewritten — (files − ceil(GB)) / GB at the 1 GB target — and the two populations are not close:

    whale 07-20…08-02  ·  1,083 files  ·  760 GB  ·  0.42 files saved per GB
    everything 08-13…08-18  ·  1,096 files  ·  17.1 GB  ·  62.6 files saved per GB

Almost the same number of files. Forty-four times less data to move. A 150× difference in return, and oldest-first spends the entire sealed budget on the wrong side of it. In 30 minutes of prod task starts: 31 SealedConsolidation claims, 20 of them on whale 07-30 (11×) and 07-31 (9×) — cells yielding 0.9 and 1.2 files/GB — and zero on 08-13 or 08-17. 6297304f / 08-17, at 303 files/GB, is the richest cell on the page and has never been claimed. This is the same measured shape as before the fix (11 of 20 then, 20 of 31 now), which is exactly why the file counts are identical.

And the pool it is competing for is already saturated. Of those 31 sealed starts, 30 were on attempts > 3 — they are re-claims of work that keeps not finishing. HotPacking is worse: 40 of 40 starts on attempts > 3, one observed at attempts = 16, with measured elapsed of 368–825 s against a 900 s deadline on a slice whose own estimate is 294 MB. Repair is on the whale’s 90 GB days logging pool exhausted — retrying this bin at 1 sort partitions. So the scheduler is not merely aimed at the low-yield population, it is aimed at it and looping on it. (The 700 s-for-294 MB HotPacking cost is not explained by anything on this page and is the next thing to measure — it is stated here as an observation, not a diagnosis.)

The shape of the fix. Rank sealed candidates by estimated files-removed per byte-rewritten rather than by age, keeping the starvation floor so nothing waits forever; and widen the converged test from “at or above target” to a band around it, so a 0.52 GB file against a 1 GB target is left alone. Both are one-line policy changes in scoring, not new machinery. Neither has been written yet — this page currently documents a diagnosis, not a shipped fix.

Where the mechanism is working. 08-19 sealed and drained 947 → 762 while still being written to; 08-18 went 500 → 445. Every project outside the whale is at 1–2 files on every day from 08-14 through 08-19 except the four named cells. On or before 08-12, 283 of 287 non-whale project-days sit at two files or fewer. The historical tail is converged; the entire remaining problem is four cells and the order in which they are reached.

Superseded by the measurement above. The paragraphs below were written against the 23:15 snapshot and are kept because the reasoning chain matters, but their conclusion — that 2efbcf5 would unfreeze 08-13 — is now falsified by the identical counts.

The backlog this page was built to track is gone. Widening the window past 08-12 for the first time shows 944 project-days holding 2,168 files between them, and 900 of those 944 cells sit at two files or fewer. Only 15 carry ten or more, and 14 of the 15 are not fragmented at all — they are the whale’s 07-20…08-02, running 0.42–1.0 GB per file against a 1 GB target. A 91 GB day should be 116 files. The single genuinely fragmented historical cell in the entire tail is whale / 08-09, 11 files for 0.9 GB. Every remaining problem on this page lives in the last seven days.

08-13 is starved by partitions that need no work at all. It has read exactly 167 for four snapshots, six days after sealing, and the cause is now measured rather than inferred. Over 25 minutes of prod task starts: 20 SealedConsolidation claims, 11 of them on whale 07-30 / 07-31 / 08-01, and zero on 08-13. Whale 07-30 alone was claimed 7 times with attempts climbing 1 → 7, at 4.44 GB estimated per unit, exceeding its 900 s deadline every time. Those partitions are already correctly packed — 103 files at 0.52 GB each against a 1 GB target.

The defect is in candidate selection, and it is the tag mistake this codebase has now made twice. select_coordinator_compaction_candidates gated its converged-file skip behind !has_unsorted, and is_sorted_run reads a tag only the OPTIMIZE path writes — 1,593 of 1,648 prod add actions carry none. So has_unsorted was true for effectively every partition and if add.size >= target { continue } was unreachable: a partition admitted for holding two small files then selected its half-gigabyte converged ones and re-sorted them. The admission test was already fixed to be size-only for exactly this reason (“SIZE only. Sortedness belongs to Repair”); selection never got the same fix. Shipped as 2efbcf5.

The ordering fix was working — that is what exposed this. Oldest-first starvation landed at 21:15 and correctly aimed sealed capacity at the oldest debt; the oldest debt just happened to be fourteen whale days that need nothing. Which also retires the hypothesis this page published earlier today: 08-13 is not cursed by its date. 08-14 (52), 08-15 (47) and 08-16 (67) converged because they were reached before the ordering changed, and 08-13 is simply not the oldest thing in the queue.

Where a day does start, it finishes. 08-18 has gone 979 → 798 → 501 across three snapshots, and the open partition’s smaller tenants collapsed in the same six hours (98fdd4f3 117 → 19, be87ebc1 65 → 17, d062e010 45 → 21). The mechanism works; what fails is selection. 08-17 is the counter-example still outstanding: 396 → 381, with 6297304f holding 274 of them and moving by a single file in twelve hours.

The rollup stall behind this has been fixed. Base rollup peaked at 88,618 pending units and is now ~7,600. One defect caused it: the byte estimate excluded only files entirely disjoint from a slice, then counted every remaining file whole. Maintenance files span a day, so a ten-minute slice estimated exactly what a day-wide slice did — and split_time_task, which bisects whatever “does not fit”, produced halves estimating the same, eleven times over: 87,000 units across 204 real cells, 427 per cell, 26,109 of them at the one-minute floor. Fixed by scaling each file’s contribution to the share of its own span the slice covers.

A caution about this page’s own numbers. Earlier today a companion tool reported shipbubble as 0/14 days sorted. It was wrong: pyarrow’s S3 client sends x-amz-checksum-mode, which OVH rejects, so every footer read failed — and the tool summed None as false. Re-measured, it is 9/9. The counts here come from Delta Add actions and are unaffected, but the lesson generalises: a three-valued probe must never be folded into a boolean at the summing step, or a broken measurement reads as a broken system.
