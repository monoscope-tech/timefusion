## Assessment

### 1. The goals have not moved yet

The plan's aim is lower CPU/IO per accepted event, and the user-facing symptom is slow reads. Both depend on the
rollup hit rate and on dedup skips, and neither changed this week:

| Day | 09-20 | 09-21 | 09-22 | 09-23 | 09-24 | 09-25 | 09-26 | 09-27 (partial) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rollup hit rate % | 4.0 | 2.3 | 1.4 | 3.1 | 1.5 | 3.2 | 1.5 | 0.3 |

A fair share of 09-26 repaired regressions introduced in the same 48 hours: DataFusion 55 broke `now()` routing,
release 2 caused OOM kills, and packed repairs went on and then off.

### 2. W31 is the only lever for the hit rate

W21 settled this. Hashes-only UPDATEs move the physical witness on every today-window slice, and carrying the witness
recovers 0.7% (`rollup_carry_eligible_rows_total` 6,585 of 920,323 version rows). Most of those UPDATEs hit rows still
in the MemBuffer. The fix is a logical or captured source view (Stage 2), not a carry.

The handover's first open item still points at the refuted fix: "fix = carry the witness (W20)".

### 3. Dedup is the bigger cost

- W7: dedup scanned **899 GB/h** against **75 GB/h** for base rollups.
- W22: this is steady state. Each sealed day is re-deduplicated for about four days, and restarts re-inflate the queue.
- Compaction now removes more duplicate rows each day (10 M → 81 M rows/day over the week), so the lane does real work.
  The read path does not benefit, because 98.7% of eligible scans are denied the skip.

If the aim is less total work, Stage 5 (fewer repeat dedups; certification that survives changes) belongs right after
W31, not under "conditional".

### 4. Staging (W30) is the cheapest unblocker

It is a small config change, and it unblocks three things:

- W7 real-S3 unit costs.
- The capacity question below.
- A rehearsal of the W29 and W27 backfills before they compete with prod maintenance.

The capacity question: in the 72 hours this week with ≥300 tasks due, the container used a median of **26 of 48
cores** (peak 39). Over the same period the maintenance CPU-token budget sat at **66 of 66**. Since `d9f00cce` (09-24)
a unit costs one token per 64 MiB of decode, so the tokens count bytes in flight, not cores. CPU admission refusals
went from 24–83 k/day before 09-24 to 1.5 M on 09-25.

The cap was deliberate: 65 concurrent rebuild scans had starved client queries at the object store. Only a staging run
at a higher cap can show whether the store or the cap binds. That run should measure store latency and client query
latency.

### 5. Deploy cadence works against measurement

- About 12 deploys on 09-26. W16 counted 65 process starts in 7.8 days.
- Each start resets `timefusion_stats`, the rollup coverage map and in-flight units, and re-inflates the dedup queue.
- The workstreams sheet says to batch changes into coherent releases. On 09-26 releases went out per stream.
- Every deploy's first new task fails with `No such container`, because CapRover updates the service twice. That costs
  1.6 s of unavailability per deploy (W25).

### 6. Decision conflict to resolve before W29

- The 09-27 owner decisions say a **7-day** backfill "for both spec changes".
- The W29 row says a **31-day** backfill, with v4 replacing v3 and v3/1h_v2 dropped once v4 covers.

If v3 is dropped after only 7 days of v4, 8–31-day windows lose rollup coverage. Choose one of these:

- Backfill v4 for 31 days.
- Keep v3 serving the older ranges until v4 has 31 days.
